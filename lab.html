The Lab

Two adjoining suites on the second floor of the Service Memorial Institute, being rebuilt as one working unit for human vascular studies and the sample work that goes with them.

The space

The VSPR Lab occupies two adjoining suites at 470 North Charter Street, in a building that opened in 1926 and still carries its original concrete structure overhead. The remodel takes roughly 1,100 square feet of older laboratory space and rebuilds it as eight connected rooms.

The arrangement follows the way a study visit actually runs. A participant arrives from the corridor, changes in a private room, and is tested in one of two clinical research bays. Blood samples move a few steps down an internal passage to the wet lab rather than across a building. Consent and interviews happen in their own room, away from the equipment and the noise.

Two walls are doing something specific. The walls that separate Clinical Research Bay 1 from the wet lab and from the changing room are built as a rated acoustic assembly, insulated and carried all the way to the underside of the concrete deck, so a measurement in the bay is far less exposed to the rest of the suite.

Room by room

224

Wet Lab

Blood sample processing, point-of-care analyses, and ELISAs. Casework wraps three walls with three seated work positions built into it. A four-foot biosafety cabinet with UV, two undermount sinks, purified water at the bench, an emergency eye wash, a glassware drying rack, and refrigerated storage.

224A

Clinical Research Bay 1

The lab's primary testing bay. Its two new walls are built as a rated acoustic assembly, which quiets the bay against the wet lab and the changing room. Wall-mounted display, privacy curtain, and a changing room immediately next door.

224B

Storage

Consumables, supplies, and equipment carts, kept out of the testing and bench areas.

224C

Passage

The internal connection between the wet lab and the clinical side, lined with six lockers for participants' personal belongings and a bench. Samples and people move between the two halves of the lab without going back out to the corridor.

224D

Changing Room

A private place to change before a visit, with a bench, hooks, lockers, and a pocket door that opens straight into Bay 1.

220

Lab

Exercise and metabolic testing, and the shared working room. Casework runs along two walls with two seated work positions built into it, alongside adjustable-height desks, monitors, a sink run, and a glassboard for working through an analysis with whoever is in the room.

220A

Clinical Research Bay 2

The second testing bay. A patient chair, a monitor on a wall mount, blackout shades, an accessible sink, and a ceiling-tracked privacy curtain.

220B

Interview Room

Where consent, questionnaires, and interviews happen, away from the equipment. A conference table, seating, a glassboard, and a shade on the window.

Shared cell laboratory

Bardeen 280

The cellular work happens in Bardeen 280, a shared wet laboratory a short walk from the suites. Cell culture, live-cell imaging, and bioenergetic phenotyping are all run there, on a Nikon Ji and a Seahorse XF Mini.

Keeping the two apart is deliberate. Sample processing stays beside the participant, in Wet Lab 224, while the longer cell experiments sit in a room built and shared for that purpose.

Details

Drawn into the remodel rather than added later.

  • Acoustic separationThe two new walls around Clinical Research Bay 1 are a rated assembly. Insulated studs, an extra layer of board on the bay side, carried to the underside of the concrete deck, specified at STC 50 against a tested assembly.
  • OverheadThe 1926 concrete joists and beams stay exposed, with acoustic clouds hung below them in the testing bays and the shared lab.
  • CaseworkBuilt-in cabinetry wraps three walls of the wet lab and two walls of Lab 220, with five seated work positions set into it. Nurse servers, a mobile cart, a glassware rack, and lockers are all part of the millwork rather than furniture brought in later.
  • Participant routeChanging room, lockers, and a private passage, so a participant never has to cross the bench area to reach a testing bay.
  • Chairside supportNurse servers built into both bays and a mobile cart, so sample handling happens where the participant already is.
  • WaterPurified water at the wet lab bench, undermount sinks in both suites, and an emergency eye wash on each side.
  • PrivacyCeiling-tracked curtains in the bays, blackout and filtering shades at the windows, and a dedicated interview room.
