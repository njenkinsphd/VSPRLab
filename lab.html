The Lab

Two adjoining suites on the second floor of the Service Memorial Institute, being rebuilt as one working unit for human vascular studies and cell work.

The space

The VSPR Lab occupies two adjoining suites at 470 North Charter Street, in a building that opened in 1926 and still carries its original concrete structure overhead. The remodel takes roughly 1,100 square feet of older laboratory space and rebuilds it as eight connected rooms.

The arrangement follows the way a study visit actually runs. A participant arrives from the corridor, changes in a private room, and is tested in one of two clinical research bays. Blood and cell samples move a few steps down an internal passage to the wet lab rather than across a building. Consent and interviews happen in their own room, away from the equipment and the noise.

One wall is doing something specific. Clinical Research Bay 2 sits behind a sound-rated partition, so a vascular measurement in one bay is not disturbed by whatever is happening in the rest of the suite.

Room by room

224

Wet Lab

Bench space for the cellular and mitochondrial work. A four-foot biosafety cabinet with UV, two undermount sinks, purified water at the bench, an emergency eye wash, a glassware drying rack, and refrigerated storage.

224A

Clinical Research Bay 2

The second testing bay, built behind a sound-rated partition so a measurement here is not disturbed by work elsewhere in the suite. Wall-mounted display, privacy curtain, and a changing room immediately next door.

224B

Storage

Consumables, supplies, and equipment carts, kept out of the testing and bench areas.

224C

Passage

The internal connection between the wet lab and the clinical side, lined with participant lockers and a bench. Samples and people move between the two halves of the lab without going back out to the corridor.

224D

Changing Room

A private place to change before a visit, with a bench, hooks, lockers, and a pocket door that opens straight into Bay 2.

220

Lab

The shared working room. Adjustable-height desks, monitors, a sink and casework run, and a glassboard for working through an analysis with whoever is in the room.

220A

Clinical Research Bay 1

The primary testing bay. A patient chair, a monitor on a wall mount, blackout shades, an accessible sink, and a ceiling-tracked privacy curtain.

220B

Interview Room

Where consent, questionnaires, and interviews happen, away from the equipment. A conference table, seating, a glassboard, and a shade on the window.

Details

Drawn into the remodel rather than added later.

  • Acoustic separationClinical Research Bay 2 is enclosed by a sound-rated partition, tested at STC 50, so two studies can run at once without one hearing the other.
  • OverheadThe 1926 concrete joists and beams stay exposed, with acoustic clouds hung below them in the testing bays and the shared lab.
  • Participant routeChanging room, lockers, and a private passage, so a participant never has to cross the bench area to reach a testing bay.
  • Chairside supportNurse servers built into both bays and a mobile cart, so sample handling happens where the participant already is.
  • WaterPurified water at the wet lab bench, undermount sinks in both suites, and an emergency eye wash on each side.
  • PrivacyCeiling-tracked curtains in the bays, blackout and filtering shades at the windows, and a dedicated interview room.

Room names, numbers, and dimensions are taken from the University of Wisconsin–Madison construction documents for project PRJ-26-006197, Remodel Lab Spaces, Service Memorial Institute, issued October 2026.
