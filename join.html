Join the Lab

The lab is building out at UW–Madison and is recruiting at the postdoctoral and graduate level.

What the lab works on

One question in several forms. Adverse childhood experiences raise cardiovascular risk decades before any disease appears, and the vessel wall is where that risk first becomes measurable. The lab studies how stress, from early life and from daily life, gets into the endothelium, and what can be done about it while people are still young enough for it to matter.

The levers are behavioral and biological. Sleep, diet, and physical activity are measured as everyday exposures and then manipulated experimentally to test whether they actually carry the effect. Mitochondrial energetic and redox function is where we look for the biology that connects the two. Projects move between measurement in daily life, controlled challenges and interventions in the lab, and cellular work on blood from the same participants.

Working across those scales is the point rather than a complication. A trainee here is expected to be comfortable on both the human and the bench side.

Postdoctoral Fellow

For someone who wants to work across human vascular physiology and cellular bioenergetics rather than one or the other. Projects run from vascular testing in people through to endothelial cell culture, live-cell imaging, and mitochondrial phenotyping.

There is no open search at the moment. Candidates bringing their own fellowship funding are still welcome to write, and one direction that could open extends this work into pregnancy, on sleep health across pregnancy in women with histories of early adversity.

Research Nurse / Nurse Phlebotomist

Supporting human studies in the clinical research bays. Venipuncture and sample collection during testing visits, participant monitoring and safety, and the clinical side of protocols that run from early morning through a full testing day.

Samples are processed a few steps away in the lab's own wet lab, so the role sits close to the science rather than at the end of it.

PhD or MS Student

Through the Department of Kinesiology graduate program. Students learn human vascular measurement, behavioral assessment, and bench work, and carry a project of their own.

Email before applying. The application asks for an area of specialization and a potential advisor, and a short conversation first makes both easier.

What you would learn to do

  • In peopleFlow-mediated dilation and conduit artery blood flow by vascular ultrasound, pulse wave analysis and central hemodynamics, and acute stress and mixed meal challenge protocols.
  • BehaviorWrist actigraphy and home sleep measurement, dietary assessment, and behavioral interventions run as experiments rather than as advice.
  • At the benchBlood processing, ELISAs, and point-of-care analyses in the lab's own wet lab, beside the testing bays.
  • Cells and mitochondriaEndothelial cell culture, live-cell imaging, mitochondrial membrane potential, and extracellular flux analysis for bioenergetic phenotyping.

Applying to the graduate program

Research-based MS and PhD, Department of Kinesiology.

  1. Email first. A paragraph on what you want to study and your CV. I can tell you whether I am taking a student in that cycle before you pay an application fee.
  2. Apply through the UW Graduate School and name Exercise Physiology as your area of specialization. The departmental instructions are on the Kinesiology admissions page.
  3. Submit a personal statement of one to two pages, three letters of recommendation, and unofficial transcripts. International applicants whose instruction was not in English also submit proof of English proficiency.
