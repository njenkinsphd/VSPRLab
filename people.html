Principal Investigator

Nathaniel D. M. Jenkins, PhD, FAHA

Director, VSPR Lab

Nathaniel Jenkins, PhD, FAHA, is an Associate Professor of Kinesiology and Population Health Sciences, a faculty member of the UW Cardiovascular Research Center and the Wisconsin Nathan Shock Center, and directs the Vascular Stress Physiology and Resilience (VSPR) Lab. He completed his PhD in 2016 and spent a total of 10 years at Oklahoma State University (Health and Human Performance and Nutritional Sciences; 4 years) and the University of Iowa (Health and Human Physiology and Psychological and Brain Sciences; 6 years) as an Assistant and Associate Professor.

His lab studies how psychosocial and environmental stressors, including adverse childhood experiences, shape blood vessel health across the lifespan. By pairing human vascular physiology, cellular and mitochondrial biology, and behavioral observation and manipulation, the lab examines what drives cardiometabolic disease risk in high-risk populations before disease becomes clinically apparent. The lab measures stress, sleep, dietary behavior, and physical activity as everyday lifestyle-related exposures that shape vascular and metabolic function, and also manipulates these experimentally to test causal mechanisms in humans.

This research is currently supported by the NIH (NHLBI/NIA) and the American Heart Association. Jenkins is a Fellow of the American Heart Association, a Consulting Editor for the American Journal of Physiology–Heart and Circulatory Physiology, and a Standing Member of the NIH Center for Scientific Review’s Biological Processes and Social-Ecological Factors Study Section.

  • Manoela Lima Oliveira, PhD

    Postdoctoral Fellow

    Dr. Manoela Lima Oliveira is a registered dietitian and nutrition researcher who earned her PhD in Human Nutrition from the University of Illinois Chicago, where she worked under the guidance of Dr. Lisa Tussing-Humphreys. Her research explored the intersections of early-onset colorectal cancer prevention, time-restricted eating patterns, mindfulness-based interventions, and the complex relationships between dietary quality, chronic psychosocial stress, and gut microbiome composition.

    With an academic foundation between both U.S. and Brazilian institutions, Dr. Oliveira has made substantial contributions to the scientific literature through peer-reviewed publications and presentations at leading conferences. Her work has received prestigious recognition, including a Predoctoral Fellowship at the NCI T32 University of Illinois Chicago’s Cancer Education and Career Development Program, a National Cancer Institute (NCI) travel award, the Best Poster award at the Dr. Gary Kruh Cancer Research Symposium, and the American Health Sciences Achievement Award from the Kinesiology & Nutrition Doctorate Program.

    Dr. Lima Oliveira joined the laboratory as a Postdoctoral Scholar in 2025. In this role, she has continued her research on chronic psychosocial stress and its health implications while expanding her focus to include Dr. Jenkins' work investigating the biological and behavioral mechanisms through which Adverse Childhood Experiences influence diverse health outcomes. She is currently supported by an American Heart Association Postdoctoral Research Supplement to Promote Diversity in Science (2025-2027).

  • Laura Schwager, BA

    Research Associate

    Laura graduated from the University of Iowa with a bachelor's degree in Design Anthropology, a self-directed program of study focused on global development at the intersection of environmental science, human behavior, and public health. Laura joined the lab in 2021. As the Research Associate, she manages lab operations, coordinates and leads research projects, and contributes to the development and implementation of protocols supporting the lab's central research focus: elucidating the biological mechanisms linking childhood psychosocial stress to premature cardiometabolic disease risk, with an emphasis on understanding pathways that inform prevention and intervention.

  • Mark Flores

    PhD Candidate

    Mark is a fourth-year PhD candidate in Health and Human Physiology. Mark completed his Bachelor of Science degrees in Chemistry & Kinesiology at McDaniel College in Westminster, Maryland. Mark’s primary research interests include the study of psychosocial and sociocultural factors contributing to health outcomes, particularly as it relates to health disparities among underrepresented populations. Mark is currently leading a project examining the role of daily stress processes in ethnicity-related differences in vascular function and metabolism. Mark is currently supported by the Lulu Merle Johnson Recruitment Fellowship from the UI Graduate College and was named an American Heart Association Scholar in the National Hispanic Latino Cardiovascular Collaborative (NHLLC) for the 2025-2026 program year. Recently, Mark accepted a position as a Program Consultant with the American Heart Association.

  • Kylee West

    PhD Candidate

    Kylee is a fourth-year PhD candidate in Health and Human Physiology. Kylee completed her Bachelor of Science in Exercise and Sports Science at Campbell University. She completed her Master of Science in Kinesiology at Georgia Southern University where she studied the effects of exercise interventions on cardiovascular and motor performance outcomes in aging adults with and without chronic diseases. Since arriving at Iowa, Kylee has diversified her skillset to include in vitro endothelial cell experiments and is exploring the effects of modifiable lifestyle behaviors (particularly sleep) that can mitigate the risk of cardiovascular and metabolic diseases.

  • Sequoia Ernst, BS

    MS Student

    Sequoia is a first-year master’s student in the Kinesiology: Exercise Physiology track. He completed his undergraduate education at Florida State University where he studied exercise physiology. During this time, he assisted Dr. Robert Hickner’s research on the influence of NOX-derived reactive oxygen species on vascular function through FSU’s UROP program. After completing UROP, he transitioned into Dr. Joseph Watso’s Laboratory, where he researched cardiometabolic responses to hyperpnea and body composition influences on blood pressure variability. He’s excited to continue studying vascular function in his graduate work at UW Madison.
